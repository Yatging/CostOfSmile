## Field visit

I visited the opening of **Microwave International New Media Arts Festival 2026 — The Science of Immateriality** at Hong Kong City Hall on 30 October 2026.

This entry is different from the online references elsewhere in the archive: I encountered the work in person and documented both the interaction space and its exhibition wall text.

**Light Years of Hope / 希望光年** is presented by **Miu Ling Lam**, in collaboration with **Cody Chow**. A current festival report describes the interaction as an invitation for viewers to speak a wish into the work; the wish is then translated into changing light and sound in a xenon plasma ring.

## What I observed

The work is visually compact but spatially strong. A small luminous ring sits low on a pedestal while the surrounding area is saturated in red light. The technological object is not presented as a screen full of data. Instead, the system receives something intangible — a spoken wish — and converts it into a physical, perceptible event.

That translation is what interests me most:

**private / invisible input → computational process → light + sound → shared physical experience**

The installation makes a technical system emotionally legible without showing the audience the machinery as an information diagram.

## Why it matters to Cost of Smile

This offers a useful model for the direction Prof. Lam suggested in our supervision meeting: do not simply put existing evidence on the wall; **translate the underlying experience into an artistic event**.

My materials could follow a similar logic:

- aligner-change calendar → rhythm, pulse, timing or visual progression;
- appointment messages → notification rhythm / waiting / interruption;
- wear-time rules → a timed interaction;
- bodily inconvenience → an action that must be repeated by the viewer;
- treatment data → light, sound, movement or another perceptible state.

The strongest connection is not the use of light itself. It is the decision to transform an invisible or abstract input into a simple sensory output.

This reinforces the direction of **Toothbrush Reveal**: the audience should not merely read that orthodontic treatment contains hidden labour; an interaction can make that hidden layer emerge through action.

## Formal method to borrow

- reduce a complex technical system to one clear interaction;
- translate abstract information into a sensory event;
- keep the interface physically simple;
- allow the viewer's own input to complete the work;
- use atmosphere to frame a small object as an experience.

## Caution

I should not imitate the red lighting, plasma ring or wish-input mechanic simply because the work is visually memorable.

For Cost of Smile, the interaction needs to come directly from orthodontic actions and records. The useful lesson is **translation**, not visual quotation.
