## Work / context — 作品与背景

The Royal College of Art's 2023 graduate page presents Synnuck's *Monument to a Perfect Smile*, a chipped and slumped anthropomorphic tooth made with Jesmonite and puff binder. It also lists *Not even by the skin of my teeth*, an extracted-tooth figure alongside banana peels, and *Get your teeth into it*. In his statement Synnuck discusses idealised bodily images, social power, self-regulation, platform-mediated visibility and the ways people come to monitor and reshape themselves.

## What can inform Cost of Smile? — 与本项目的联系

For *Cost of Smile*, Synnuck supports the shift from **financial cost** to **social cost**: the smile as a promise of acceptance, attractiveness or improved social position. Where Synnuck renders a failed monument and anthropomorphised tooth, an aligner archive might emphasise the time and effort required to pursue that ideal. 中文：把“理想笑容”的社会期待变成不稳定甚至失败的纪念碑。

## A specific experiment to try — 可执行的借鉴

For a research comparison, place an uncoloured, numbered aligner beside a deliberately picturesque painted aligner and document the contrast between clinical normalisation and idealised scenic appearance. A short text can ask, 'Who decided what this landscape should become?' Do not quote a clinical verdict from this artistic reference.

## Distinction / limits — 需要避免的误读

The RCA statement explores a broad set of bodily and digital pressures, **not evidence that all orthodontic patients experience peer pressure**. Avoid imposing a single explanation on the artist's practice or on your own medical choice.
