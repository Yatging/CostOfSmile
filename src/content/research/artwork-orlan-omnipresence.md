## Work / medium

**Omniprésence** (21 November 1993) is part of ORLAN's surgical performance practice. Centre Pompidou catalogues the work as a colour video performance; LACMA also holds photographic documentation from the operation.

## What the work does

ORLAN does not hide the medical procedure behind a polished before-and-after result. The intervention itself becomes public, performative and mediated. The body is treated as something that can be deliberately redesigned while the conditions of that redesign remain visible.

## Why it matters to Cost of Smile

Orthodontics is far less extreme, but the structural question is related: bodily change is planned through professional intervention and judged against an imagined future appearance.

The useful reference is not shock. It is ORLAN's refusal to let the **final transformed body erase the labour and system that produced it**.

For Cost of Smile, the aligners, stage numbers, appointments and failed attachments can keep the transformation process visible rather than collapsing everything into a final smile.

## Formal method to borrow

- foreground process instead of before/after;
- keep the apparatus of bodily modification visible;
- treat documentation as part of the artwork;
- question who defines the desirable future body.

## Caution

ORLAN's work engages elective surgery, feminist body art and deliberate spectacle. Invisalign should not be exaggerated into an equivalent form of violence. The reference is conceptual and formal, not a claim of equal stakes.
