Conrad's concept of **medicalisation** describes processes through which aspects of human life become defined and treated as medical problems.

His later work is especially useful because medicalisation is not presented as something produced by doctors alone. Conrad also discusses wider forces such as biotechnology, pharmaceutical industries, health systems and patients as consumers.

## How this informs Cost of Smile

Orthodontic treatment can involve legitimate functional concerns while also overlapping with aesthetic improvement and consumer choice.

The useful question is therefore not:

> Is orthodontics “really” medical or “really” cosmetic?

Instead, the project asks how dental difference is described through diagnosis, bite, risk and oral function while the same intervention may also be purchased as an improvement of appearance.

## Limits / caution

Medicalisation is a **lens**, not a verdict. The project should not imply that the dentist's clinical judgement was simply a sales narrative or that orthodontic treatment is inherently unnecessary.
