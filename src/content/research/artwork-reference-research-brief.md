The artwork-reference search is organised around five clusters. The individual case studies now sit inside the Research archive rather than remaining only a future task.

## 1. Medical images, institutions and bodily traces

- **Mona Hatoum — *Corps étranger***: transforming medical imaging into spatial experience.
- **Damien Hirst — *Pharmacy***: using the visual system of medicine and pharmaceutical display as installation.
- **Jo Spence — *The Picture of Health?***: reclaiming representation of the treated body.

## 2. Personal data, calendars and self-tracking

- **Tehching Hsieh — *One Year Performance 1980–1981***: time, routine, administrative evidence and self-surveillance.
- **Giorgia Lupi & Stefanie Posavec — *Dear Data***: translating mundane personal records into human visual language.
- **Nicholas Felton — *The 2009 Feltron Annual Report***: turning a year of personal data into institutional graphic form.

## 3. Medical imaging, 3D models and the digital body

- **Heather Dewey-Hagborg — *Stranger Visions***: bodily residue → data → computational model → physical object.
- **Jes Fan — *Gut / Cross Section***: translating scanned internal anatomy into sculptural material.

## 4. Beauty, bodily ideals and deliberate modification

- **ORLAN — *Omniprésence***: keeping the apparatus and process of bodily modification visible rather than presenting only a polished outcome.

## 5. Repetitive care, training and designed routines

- **Lucy McRae — *Institute of Isolation***: turning bodily conditioning into an immersive visual and institutional system.

## Research principle

The aim is not to find artworks that “look like Invisalign.” The useful question is:

> **What artistic method can translate my existing archive into an experience rather than an information wall?**

Each case-study page records the medium, method, specific relevance, limitations, external sources and image credit.
