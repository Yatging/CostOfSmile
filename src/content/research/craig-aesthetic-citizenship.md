Craig's research on **perfect smiles and aesthetic citizenship** shows how dental appearance can acquire social meanings beyond oral health.

Her historical argument is especially useful for thinking about the smile as something connected to social belonging, respectability, class and ideas of proper care.

## How this informs Cost of Smile

This supports the developing idea of **social cost**.

The decision to straighten teeth may be experienced as personal, but ideas of what counts as a “good” smile are socially produced. Peer experience and aesthetic expectation can therefore be treated as part of the treatment environment rather than as background noise.

## Limits / caution

Craig's research is specifically about the **United States**. It cannot be treated as evidence that Hong Kong or mainland Chinese contexts operate in the same way.

If this part remains central, the project still needs Hong Kong / East Asian research and, ideally, locally grounded material.
