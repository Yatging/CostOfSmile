## Work / medium

**Corps étranger** (1994) is a mixed-media installation built around a cylindrical structure, video projection, sound and endoscopic imagery of Hatoum's own body. Centre Pompidou describes the work as an installation in which the spectator enters a symbolic reconstructed body and confronts images produced through a medical instrument.

## What the work does

Instead of displaying medical imagery as neutral documentation, Hatoum enlarges and spatialises it. Internal bodily images become difficult to recognise and physically surround the viewer. The clinical image is therefore transformed into an unsettling encounter with intimacy, vulnerability and the scientific gaze.

## Why it matters to Cost of Smile

This is a direct warning against simply pinning dental scans or progress photographs on a wall. The source image can remain medical, but the **artistic form can change how it is experienced**.

For Cost of Smile, scans or ClinCheck material could be cropped, projected, layered, enlarged or spatially integrated so that the viewer encounters the body as measured data rather than merely reading “before / after” evidence.

## Formal method to borrow

- transform clinical imagery rather than merely display it;
- use scale and spatial position to alter how a medical image is read;
- make the viewer physically negotiate the image;
- retain ambiguity instead of explaining everything with captions.

## Caution

The project should not imitate Hatoum's endoscopic spectacle or treat bodily interiority as inherently shocking. My material is orthodontic and administrative; its strength may lie in repetition, planning and measurement rather than visceral imagery.
