## Work / context — 作品与背景

Museum Oberschönenfeld's 11 July 2024 announcement identifies *Shiny Watzmann* as an idealised mountain sculpture in Huber's *Die Bibliothek* installation, made with **dental plaster, stainless steel, wire ropes and lighting**, dated 2023–2024. The museum's photograph is credited to **Leonie Felle**. The white suspended peak makes a material strongly associated with dental practice appear monumental and geological.

## What can inform Cost of Smile? — 与本项目的联系

Huber demonstrates how **material provenance and formal presentation** can transform clinical associations. In *Cost of Smile*, clear shells rather than dental plaster provide the mountain. Lighting and shadow must make the shell's faint contours readable without hiding the dental source. 中文：医学材料与山峦造型之间的形式转换，是这个参考真正有用的地方。

## A specific experiment to try — 可执行的借鉴

Make three low-angle photographs of the same retired-aligner arrangement: diffused front light, grazing side light and backlight. Keep the stage labels constant and record which lighting reveals the transparent ridges as 'geology' rather than an indistinct pile. Preserve original trays without heat or paint at this stage.

## Distinction / limits — 需要避免的误读

**The original sculpture uses dental plaster, not Invisalign aligners**, and the mountain is idealised, not a tracked bodily change. The lessons concern materials, elevation and lighting, rather than an identical treatment narrative. Link to the museum's credited photograph instead of republishing it.
