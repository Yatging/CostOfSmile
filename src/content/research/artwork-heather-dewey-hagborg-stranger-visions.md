## Work / medium

For **Stranger Visions**, Dewey-Hagborg collected discarded biological traces such as hair, gum and cigarette butts, extracted DNA, analysed genetic information and used computational processes to generate and 3D print speculative portraits.

## What the work does

The project turns an almost invisible bodily trace into a highly visible object. It also makes the translation chain explicit: residue → data → interpretation → digital model → physical output.

The artist frames the work critically around forensic DNA phenotyping, biological surveillance and genetic determinism.

## Why it matters to Cost of Smile

My used aligners are also bodily traces, while intraoral scans and ClinCheck models translate my teeth into digital data.

This suggests a useful contrast:

**physical residue → medical scan → digital model → planned future body**

The project can make that translation visible instead of treating 3D files as neutral technical material.

## Formal method to borrow

- expose the chain between bodily trace and digital representation;
- move between physical and digital forms;
- use 3D output as interpretation rather than “truth”;
- make invisible data materially present.

## Caution

Stranger Visions is centrally about DNA, surveillance and forensic prediction. Cost of Smile should not import those stakes artificially. The relevant lesson is the **translation of bodily evidence into data and form**.
