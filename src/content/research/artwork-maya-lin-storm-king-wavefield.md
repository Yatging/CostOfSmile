## Work / context — 作品与背景

Maya Lin's *Storm King Wavefield* (2009) is a large-scale earthwork at Storm King Art Center. The studio describes seven rows of rolling earth-and-grass waves, transforming the movement of water into landform. Its precise profile is legible from multiple viewpoints, including walking within the waves. The project is a **formal and spatial reference**, not an artwork about dentistry.

## What can inform Cost of Smile? — 与本项目的联系

The principle to borrow is **translation of a process into topography**, not copying a recognisable mountain silhouette. Invisalign stages also describe movement across time. A landscape could be built from a progression of tooth-contour records, allowing viewers to discover a measured movement in an apparently natural form. 中文：不是把牙套装饰成山，而是把时间中的牙齿移动转成地貌。

## A specific experiment to try — 可执行的借鉴

Make a sequence of contour models labelled with actual stage numbers. Compare a side elevation (landscape) and overhead view (dental arch). Ensure that stage differences are genuinely observable: if the differences are too small, use clearly disclosed spacing or tracing rather than pretending there has been dramatic real movement.

## Distinction / limits — 需要避免的误读

Lin's monumental earthwork operates at an entirely different scale and environmental context. A gallery tabletop installation cannot claim its immersive effects without testing. The analogy is morphological and procedural only.
