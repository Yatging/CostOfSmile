## Work / medium

Jes Fan's recent works include **Gut** (2024), described by Empty Gallery as a 3D print of the artist's internal organ combined with fibreglass, resin, pigment, glass and metal, as well as **Cross Section** works developed from medical imaging of the artist's body.

## What the work does

The scan is only a starting point. Internal anatomy is translated through 3D printing, resin, pigment, glass and sculptural framing until it becomes something between anatomical evidence, landscape and constructed object.

This is important because the work preserves a relation to bodily data without leaving the data in its default clinical visual language.

## Why it matters to Cost of Smile

If I obtain intraoral scans or treatment models, I do not need to display the software interface or raw scan directly.

Fan's work suggests a chain:

**scan → selection → transformation → material form**

For Cost of Smile, a tooth model, attachment geometry or intermediate stage could become a sculptural or digital structure while still retaining its origin in my own measured body.

## Formal method to borrow

- use medical imaging as source material rather than final image;
- translate data between digital and physical media;
- allow the source body to become abstract while remaining traceable;
- combine technical accuracy with material ambiguity.

## Caution

Fan's work is deeply connected to questions of biology, identity, race, gender and ecology. Those contexts should not be borrowed casually. My useful connection is the **method of translating medical imaging into sculpture**.
