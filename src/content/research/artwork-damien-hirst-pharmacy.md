## Work / medium

**Pharmacy** (1992) is a room-scale installation using cabinets, glass, pharmaceutical and apothecary bottles, furniture, a fly zapper, bowls and honey. White Cube records the work as an installation measuring more than six metres across; DACS documents its pharmaceutical materials and installation views.

## What the work does

Hirst does not merely place medicine inside a gallery. He recreates the **visual system and authority of a pharmacy**: ordered shelves, sterile whiteness, labelled products and a controlled display environment.

The medical-commercial space itself becomes the medium.

## Why it matters to Cost of Smile

My packaging and aligners also come from a highly designed consumer-medical system.

This case suggests that I should pay attention not only to the objects but to the **display logic that makes them appear trustworthy, scientific and purchasable**.

The numbered packages could therefore be arranged using, exaggerating or disrupting the visual language of clinical order rather than simply pinned to a context wall.

## Formal method to borrow

- reconstruct or appropriate an institutional display language;
- use repetition and categorisation spatially;
- allow consumer packaging to carry conceptual meaning;
- make the environment itself part of the argument.

## Caution

Hirst's visual language is iconic and easily imitated. A white clinical grid alone would become stylistic quotation. The form needs to grow from the specific serial logic of orthodontic stages and personal treatment records.
