## English

Three questions guide the reference study. How was the mountain made? Where must the viewer stand before the material reveals its earlier use? What can suggest a surface of water when the water itself is absent? The linked artist, collection, exhibition and garden records provide access to the original images. The project publishes its own composition drawing.

## Dental materials and bodily experience

**Stephan Huber · Shiny Watzmann (2023–2024).** Museum Oberschönenfeld identifies the idealised Watzmann as part of *Die Bibliothek*. The work incorporates dental plaster, stainless steel, wire ropes and lighting. The museum credits the photograph to Leonie Felle. A material associated with dentistry becomes legible as a mountain through a change in scale. Transparent trays present a different lighting problem. In study A, try catching the ridge with side light while allowing its supports to fall into shadow.

Museum Oberschönenfeld. (2024, July 11). *Schwäbische Galerie Oberschönenfeld: Der Allgäuer Künstler Stephan Huber zeigt „Das große Leuchten“* [Exhibition announcement for Stephan Huber’s *Das große Leuchten*]. [Museum text and photograph](https://mos.bezirk-schwaben.de/das-museum/pressemeldungen/schwaebische-galerie-oberschoenenfeld-der-allgaeuer-kuenstler-stephan-huber-zeigt-das-grosse-leuchten/)

**Devon Stackonis · Retaining Walls.** Stackonis observed dental moulds and casts to make etchings for an artist’s book installation. Her family’s labour history in Pennsylvania coal country, the legacy of extraction and her experience of chronic pain inform the work. Actions such as pulling, eroding and supporting link the body to the mine. The corresponding verbs in *Orthodontic Strata* are smaller in scale: remove, clean, replace. A diary entry placed beside its stage could bring one of these actions into view.

Stackonis, D. (n.d.). *Retaining walls* [Artist’s book installation]. [Artist’s statement and photographs](https://www.devonstackonis.com/retaining-walls)

## How a repeated unit becomes terrain

**Brian Thompson · River Journeys (2010).** Thompson traced rivers from aerial photographs and made digital templates for water-jet cutting sheet glass. Small changes in size from one sheet to the next allow the exposed edges to register the order of making. If the shells overlap too closely in study A, trace the arches at a common scale and use clear replicas to show their sequence. Disclose the sampling method and any exaggerated spacing.

Thompson, B. (2010). *River journeys* [Sculpture collection]. University of Sunderland. [Work record](https://sure.sunderland.ac.uk/id/eprint/4055/) · [Artist’s account of the process](https://sure.sunderland.ac.uk/id/eprint/4055/6/House_of_Commons_statement_BT.pdf)

**Tara Donovan · Untitled (Plastic Cups).** ICA Boston documents a large landscape assembled from plastic cups. Repetition, quantity and differences in overall height change the scale at which viewers read the cups. Three to five trays offer far fewer edges. Try a low-angle view of a short section of arch to see whether those few edges can suggest terrain.

Institute of Contemporary Art/Boston. (n.d.). *Tara Donovan*. [Museum exhibition publication](https://www.icaboston.org/publications/tara-donovan/)

**Yuken Teruya · Notice – Forest: Madison Avenue (2011).** This collection work uses a paper bag and glue. Looking into the bag reveals a tree. The opening and the packaging together determine how the viewer encounters it. Keeping an aligner packet’s sealed edge could recall the gesture of tearing it open. Whether flexible film holds a crease as paper holds a cut tree is a material question for study B.

Museum of Contemporary Art Tokyo. (n.d.). *Notice – Forest: Madison Avenue* [Collection record]. [Collection information and photograph](https://www.mot-collection-search.jp/en/shiryo/6998/)

## Two gardens and two ways to compose a view

**The rock garden at Ryōan-ji.** Kyoto’s official tourism account describes fifteen stones in raked white gravel. From the viewing platform, the stones partly conceal one another as the angle changes. The designer and precise construction date remain uncertain. The useful questions here concern how a foreground form obscures a distant one and how empty space sets the pace of looking. Leave enough space between packet and tray for the arch to emerge in plan.

Kyoto City Tourism Association. (n.d.). *Ryoan-ji Temple*. Kyoto Travel. [Garden and viewing position](https://kyoto.travel/en/destinations/ryoanji-temple/)

**Mirei Shigemori · Tōfuku-ji Hōjō Hassō Garden (1939).** Tōfuku-ji attributes the gardens around its abbot’s hall to Shigemori. In the southern garden, groups of rocks evoke islands and swirls of raked gravel suggest the sea. Reused foundation stones and moss create a checkerboard of varying density in the northern garden. Study B could compare packet pleats that curve around the ridge with parallel pleats and ask which makes viewers think of water. The attribution to Shigemori here applies specifically to Tōfuku-ji.

Tōfuku-ji. (n.d.). *Tōfuku-ji’s Hōjō “Hassō” garden* [Visitor leaflet]. [Temple guide and garden photographs](https://tofukuji.jp/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/tofukuji_hojogarden.pdf)

看参考图时，可以带着三个具体问题：作品里的山是怎样做出来的？要从哪里看，材料原来的身份才显现？如果拿掉真正的水，还能靠什么让人读出水面？下列链接通往作者、馆藏、展览机构或庭园资料。作品图请在来源页查看，本站只刊登自己的构图示意。

## 牙科材料和身体经验

**Stephan Huber · Shiny Watzmann（2023–2024）。** Museum Oberschönenfeld 的展签把这座理想化的瓦茨曼山列为装置 *Die Bibliothek* 的一部分，材料包含牙科石膏、不锈钢、钢索和灯具。馆方照片署名 Leonie Felle。这里的连接点是牙科材料跨越尺度之后仍能被读成山。A 方案更难的地方在于透明：试拍时把光给到山脊边缘，并让支撑件退入阴影。

Museum Oberschönenfeld. (2024, July 11). *Schwäbische Galerie Oberschönenfeld: Der Allgäuer Künstler Stephan Huber zeigt „Das große Leuchten“*. [馆方图文](https://mos.bezirk-schwaben.de/das-museum/pressemeldungen/schwaebische-galerie-oberschoenenfeld-der-allgaeuer-kuenstler-stephan-huber-zeigt-das-grosse-leuchten/)

**Devon Stackonis · Retaining Walls。** 艺术家以宾夕法尼亚煤矿区的家族劳动史、开采和慢性疼痛为背景，观察牙模与印模绘制蚀刻版画，再编成艺术家书装置。「拔除」「侵蚀」「支撑」这些动作让身体和矿区相遇。对口中地层，更贴近个人经验的动词是「取下」「洗净」「换副」。可选一个有日记印证的动作贴近对应层位，让观众读到塑料轮廓之外的劳动。

Stackonis, D. (n.d.). *Retaining walls* [Artist’s book installation]. [作者说明与作品照片](https://www.devonstackonis.com/retaining-walls)

## 如何把一个单位读成一片地貌

**Brian Thompson · River Journeys（2010）。** 作者从河流航拍图描线，做成数码模板，以水刀切割片玻璃。逐片改变尺寸后，叠合的玻璃把制作次序留在断面里。这给 A 方案一个可执行的后备方法：同一尺度描取牙弓，用透明副本显示先后；放大的间距与取样方法要随作品公开。

Thompson, B. (2010). *River journeys* [Sculpture collection]. University of Sunderland. [作品档案](https://sure.sunderland.ac.uk/id/eprint/4055/) · [作者制作说明](https://sure.sunderland.ac.uk/id/eprint/4055/6/House_of_Commons_statement_BT.pdf)

**Tara Donovan · Untitled (Plastic Cups)。** ICA Boston 记录了塑料杯组成的大型地貌。杯子靠数量、重复和整体高度分布改变观看尺度。三至五副牙套很难获得同样的密度；首轮可以试一小段牙弓的低角度取景，检查少数边缘是否已经足够。

Institute of Contemporary Art/Boston. (n.d.). *Tara Donovan*. [馆方展览出版物](https://www.icaboston.org/publications/tara-donovan/)

**Yuken Teruya · Notice – Forest: Madison Avenue（2011）。** 馆藏作品用纸袋和胶做成，观众向袋内看才发现树。入口与包装本身共同决定观看动作。对于塑料牙套袋，保留封边可让观众回想撕袋的手势；纸能撑起精细树形，并不能证明薄膜褶皱会定形，这一点交给 B 方案实测。

Museum of Contemporary Art Tokyo. (n.d.). *Notice – Forest: Madison Avenue* [Collection record]. [馆藏资料与照片](https://www.mot-collection-search.jp/en/shiryo/6998/)

## 两座庭园，两种构图依据

**龙安寺石庭。** 京都官方旅游资料记载十五块石头与耙过的白砾，从观看平台望去，石块会因视角而相互遮挡。庭园作者与确切建造时间未定。可借鉴的是一块前景怎样遮住一部分后景，以及空处如何调节观看节奏；给牙套和包装留出呼吸的距离，同时保留俯视揭示牙弓的机会。

Kyoto City Tourism Association. (n.d.). *Ryoan-ji Temple*. Kyoto Travel. [庭园图与观看位置](https://kyoto.travel/en/destinations/ryoanji-temple/)

**重森三玲 · 东福寺方丈八相之庭（1939）。** 东福寺将方丈四周的庭园明确归于重森三玲。南庭以成组岩石示岛、以旋转的耙砾纹示海；北庭则将旧础石和苔组织成疏密变化的方格。B 方案可比较「围绕山脚转折」与「整齐平行」两种袋褶，分别记录哪种令观众想起水。这个归属只适用于此处所述的东福寺作品。

Tōfuku-ji. (n.d.). *Tōfuku-ji’s Hōjō “Hassō” garden* [Visitor leaflet]. [寺方图解与庭园照片](https://tofukuji.jp/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/tofukuji_hojogarden.pdf)
