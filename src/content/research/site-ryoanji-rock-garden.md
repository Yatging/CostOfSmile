## Artwork / site — 作品背景

The rock garden at Ryōan-ji in Kyoto presents **15 stones on a broad expanse of raked white gravel**, enclosed by walls and experienced primarily from a viewing platform. Kyoto's tourism authority notes that its designer and exact creation date remain unknown. The raked surface stands in for a body of water without requiring literal water. This entry is categorised as a **spatial / garden precedent**, not a contemporary artist case study.

## Relevance to Cost of Smile — 关联与分析

The dry side of the proposed installation can use toothpaste or a safer dry analogue to **suggest water without liquid**. The distinction between actual mouthwash water on one side and water represented by white material on the other becomes an artistic contrast. Empty space, controlled vantage point and the visibility of dental 'rocks' may be more important than an elaborate miniature.

## Method / test to borrow — 可尝试的方法

Prototype a minimal dry landscape with only three aligner forms and a comb-raked white material on a removable tray. Test top, side and 45-degree views. Do not use real toothpaste until it has been observed drying and cracking over time.

## Limits — 区别与注意

Do not claim the garden has a single universally accepted symbolic meaning or a verified designer. A miniature homage should avoid presenting every traditional garden as interchangeable, and should acknowledge cultural differences between Chinese shanshui and Japanese karesansui.
