The project now uses **body as project** as its main organising framework.

The treatment process begins with a scan and digital model, then moves through planned stages, scheduled changes, check-ups and a projected endpoint. Each aligner represents a temporary state between the present body and a planned future body.

Chris Shilling's sociology of the body provides a useful theoretical foundation for treating the body as something that can be worked on, maintained and reconstructed as part of self-identity. This helps connect orthodontic treatment to broader questions of bodily management without making the project purely about medicine.

## How this informs Cost of Smile

Invisalign makes the “project” unusually visible:

- the body is scanned and converted into data;
- change is divided into numbered stages;
- the patient follows a timetable;
- progress is checked against a planned result;
- the future body is imagined before it exists.

This framework can connect the physical aligners, digital scans, calendar and appointment archive without forcing them into one single explanation.

## Limits / caution

The body-as-project idea can overstate individual control. Bodies are not infinitely malleable, and treatment decisions are shaped by professional advice, money, social norms and practical constraints. Patient agency therefore needs to remain in tension with medical and social systems.
