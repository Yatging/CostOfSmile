Khalid and Quiñonez examine the social history and meaning of **straight, white teeth** rather than treating that appearance as a naturally universal ideal.

The paper is useful because it connects dental aesthetics to social distinction, professional practices, media and wider cultural expectations.

## How this informs Cost of Smile

The reference supports the project's question:

> Why does a straighter smile feel worth working and paying for?

It helps shift the work from “I personally wanted better teeth” toward the social production of what an improved mouth is supposed to look like.

This can inform future research into peer pressure, advertising, clinic language and the normalisation of orthodontic treatment among people around me.

## Limits / caution

The study is located in a **North American** context. It should not be used as evidence for Hong Kong without further local research.
