## Artwork / site — 作品背景

The University of Sunderland's record documents *River Journeys* (2010), a group of sculptural works based on river mappings from Ordnance Survey maps and aerial photographs. The techniques include **water-jet-cut glass, kiln-formed glass and cast iron**. Several glass sculptures use separately cut and assembled layers that make landscape contours readable as a constructed stack.

## Relevance to Cost of Smile — 关联与分析

This is an especially helpful fabrication analogue for **stage-by-stage dental contours** if translucent plastic shells fail to produce clearly separated strata. A traced dental arch, recorded from a real stage, could be represented in accurately indexed, laser-cut transparent or matte sheets instead of altering the original aligners.

## Method / test to borrow — 可尝试的方法

Trace the same tooth feature from three documented stages, keeping the scale fixed; produce a paper or clear-sheet mockup with a labelled vertical interval. Mark the drawing as an interpretive visualisation if spacing is exaggerated. Compare it with the unaltered aligner photograph.

## Limits — 区别与注意

*River Journeys* concerns waterways, mapped geography and glass sculpture — **not** medical imagery or orthodontic records. A dental translation must acknowledge differences in scale and measurement. The archive cannot assume the user's treatment data contains clean comparable 3D files.
