## Work / context — 作品与背景

Claire Hashman founded *Hashman Bites* in 2023 after saving the aligners used in her own orthodontic treatment. The project makes jewellery and sculptural accessories from upcycled aligners, including rings, earrings, brooches and necklaces. Hashman's own account emphasises material reuse, visual play and sustainability. See the [studio's product examples](https://hashmanbites.com/) and [artist profile](https://clairehashman.com/about).

## What can inform Cost of Smile? — 与本项目的联系

**Direct material overlap:** unlike jewellery that merely resembles a tooth, Hashman's designs work with the aligner itself. The intimate object changes location, audience and meaning: from an invisible medical appliance to an accessory deliberately seen by other people. For *Cost of Smile*, that shift could reveal the tension between medically planned self-improvement, personal memory and beauty expectations. 中文：旧牙套被变成戒指后，过去在口腔内部进行的身体管理成为可展示的装饰。

## A specific experiment to try — 可执行的借鉴

Build a **non-wearable** one-tooth jewellery maquette using a cleaned, documented replica and a separate mount. Keep its stage number nearby. Compare audience readings when the object is labelled as (1) medical waste, (2) jewellery, and (3) a record of one bodily stage. Document differences without claiming them as study findings before testing.

## Distinction / limits — 需要避免的误读

This is a close precedent, **not an invention unique to Cost of Smile**. Hashman's emphasis on upcycling does not prove a general environmental benefit for any particular fabrication method. Do not assume reworked oral-contact materials are safe to wear. For the present installation, the landscape and stage sequence may be more distinctive than jewellery alone.
