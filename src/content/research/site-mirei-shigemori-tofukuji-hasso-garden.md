## Artwork / site — 作品背景

According to Tōfuku-ji's own illustrated visitor leaflet, landscape designer **Mirei Shigemori** designed the four gardens surrounding the abbot's hall in **1939**. The Southern Garden combines groups of tall rocks representing islands with swirling raked gravel suggesting rough seas; the Northern Garden reuses foundation stones among moss to make a patterned field. The temple's PDF includes photographs and plans in its explanatory guide.

## Relevance to Cost of Smile — 关联与分析

Unlike a generic rock garden, this example deliberately combines **natural reference with abstract organisation**. The proposed white toothpaste/packaging pattern could be shaped like circulating currents while the numbered aligner strata sit like islands; the stage numbers offer the rational grid of treatment against a seemingly organic landscape. 中文：医疗阶段的秩序与人工构成的自然景观之间可以形成视觉张力。

## Method / test to borrow — 可尝试的方法

Draw two alternate tabletop plans: a dry garden with curved 'water' currents around aligner peaks, and an almost clinical gridded layout. Compare how strongly each preserves treatment chronology. Use replaceable material prototypes for the surface pattern so repeat brushing does not destroy the archive.

## Limits — 区别与注意

The **Shigemori attribution applies to the Tōfuku-ji gardens**, not to Ryōan-ji. The temple's contemporary dry-garden language is a reference for composition, not a claim that orthodontic treatment follows a Zen philosophy. The PDF photographs belong to their respective rights holders; link rather than rehost.
