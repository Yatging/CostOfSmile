## Work / medium

**Dear Data** is a year-long analogue data-drawing project. Each week, Lupi and Posavec selected a theme from everyday life, collected personal observations, translated them into a hand-drawn visual system, and mailed the resulting postcard to the other person.

## What the work does

The project refuses the idea that data has to look cold or corporate. Repetition, subjectivity, handwriting and visual invention turn mundane personal observations into intimate visual narratives.

## Why it matters to Cost of Smile

This is highly relevant to the electronic aligner calendar, appointment rhythm and daily inconvenience archive.

Instead of showing screenshots, I can extract **patterns**: how many times an action occurs, when it occurs, what interrupts it, how long it lasts, or how it feels. These can then be redesigned into a visual grammar that remains personal without revealing private raw records.

Particularly relevant Dear Data themes include clocks, schedules, things bought, physical contact, complaints, food preferences, beauty products, beauty, and smiling at strangers.

## Formal method to borrow

- design a legend before designing the final visual;
- translate intimate data into marks, rather than screenshots;
- allow personal imperfection and handwriting to remain visible;
- use repeated small units to accumulate lived experience.

## Caution

A beautiful data visualisation can easily aestheticise inconvenience until the “cost” disappears. The visual system must still communicate friction, discipline or interruption rather than becoming decorative pattern alone.
