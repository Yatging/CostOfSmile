The initial proposal placed a curved panel behind the central aligner form.

It was planned to contain numbered packaging, prop money, global cost comparison, optional progress images and sticky notes describing everyday inconvenience.

## Why it was reconsidered

The wall explained many aspects of treatment but risked becoming an **information display rather than an artwork**.

At the 26 October meeting, the sticky-note approach was specifically considered insufficient: it names inconvenience but does not transform the experience into an artistic form. Directly displaying treatment photographs was also considered too literal.

The current rule is to keep contextual material restrained and to test whether one visual, digital or spatial layer can communicate more effectively than many explanatory elements.
