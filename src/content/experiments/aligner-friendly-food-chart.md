## Starting point

The idea appeared while eating pasta with a pale soy-milk-based sauce.

During clear-aligner treatment, I have gradually developed an informal sense that some foods feel more **“aligner-friendly”** than others.

My first intuitive variables were:

- **colour / transparency** — pale or clear foods feel less likely to leave visible colour;
- **sugar level** — lower-sugar foods feel easier to reconcile with oral-hygiene concerns;
- **hardness** — hard food is relevant because I have previously detached small orthodontic attachments while eating;
- **residue / stickiness** — some foods create more cleaning work before the aligners go back in;
- **time / inconvenience** — even a small snack can trigger the whole remove → eat → clean → reinsert routine.

## Possible form

This could become an **Aligner-Friendly Food Chart** rather than a conventional diet guide.

Possible visual systems:

### 1. White → dark spectrum

Foods could be arranged from transparent / white to strongly coloured.

The chart could look almost like a dental shade guide, but instead of tooth colour it maps everyday meals.

### 2. Multi-axis scoring

Each food receives a score across several experiential variables:

- colour intensity;
- sugar;
- hardness;
- stickiness / residue;
- cleaning effort;
- interruption to wear time.

The result could be a radar chart, matrix, colour field or absurdly over-engineered “medical” classification system.

### 3. A menu for an orthodontic body

The project could present a fictional menu organised not by cuisine or taste but by how much **maintenance** each food creates.

That would make eating visible as another way treatment reorganises ordinary behaviour.

## Why it may be useful

The idea belongs to **personal / bodily cost**.

It translates a very small everyday decision — “what should I eat?” — into evidence that the treatment system can enter meals, timing, cleaning and risk calculations.

It also has humour. The more seriously the chart classifies ordinary food, the more visible the absurdity of constantly thinking through treatment rules.

## Important research caution

This is currently a **personal artistic taxonomy**, not dental or nutritional advice.

Official Invisalign guidance says the aligners are removable and should normally be taken out for eating and drinking, so there are not formal food restrictions in the same way as fixed braces. The categories above therefore need to be framed as **my lived perception of maintenance, staining, hygiene and attachment risk**, not as a clinical ranking of “safe” foods.

If this becomes an artwork, any claims about sugar, staining, acidity or attachment damage should be separately researched before being presented as factual guidance.
