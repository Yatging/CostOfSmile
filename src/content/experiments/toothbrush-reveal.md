## Starting idea

The interaction was inspired by scratch cards and museum “archaeological excavation” toys in which something is gradually uncovered from an opaque layer.

Instead of giving the audience a written list of inconveniences, the viewer must perform a **repetitive dental-care gesture** before information becomes visible.

Possible hidden lines include:

- 22 HOURS / DAY
- Remove before every meal.
- Brush before putting them back.
- Change to the next stage.
- No spontaneous snacking.
- Where did I put the aligners?
- Appointment / waiting / rescheduling.
- Did I wear them long enough today?

## Concept

The audience appears to be **cleaning**, but is actually **revealing**.

Information only appears through repeated effort. This connects minor daily inconvenience to self-discipline and to the hidden labour behind an ideal smile.

## Material directions to test

**A. White scratch-off layer + modified toothbrush — current priority.**  
A short, stiff brush or a hidden rounded PET/PP scraper could preserve the brushing gesture while allowing gradual removal.

**B. Washable tempera + slightly wet hard-bristle toothbrush.**  
Closer to actual brushing and potentially resettable, but it creates wet residue and maintenance issues.

**C. Masking fluid / removable membrane.**  
An interesting skin-like material quality, but probably less suitable for repeated exhibition use.

**D. Water-soluble experimental layer.**  
A methylcellulose-based white coating could turn brushing into gradual dissolving rather than scratching, but requires testing.

A peelable rubber coating was considered less suitable because it may lift as a sheet rather than reveal information gradually.

## Minimum prototype

Produce three 10 × 10 cm samples and compare:

1. reveal quality;
2. effort and brushing duration;
3. mess and maintenance;
4. whether the gesture reads as brushing rather than scraping.
