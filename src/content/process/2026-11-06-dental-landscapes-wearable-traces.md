On **6 November 2026**, a new idea emerged from the rising and falling contours of transparent aligners: could the dental arch become a miniature three-dimensional 山水 / terrain picture? Alternatively, could individual tooth contours become rings or other personal ornaments?

The [Dental Landscapes / Wearable Traces experiment](/CostOfSmile/experiments/dental-landscapes-wearable-traces/) records these as **two separate proposed directions**:

- **Landscape:** translate bodily contours into ridges, hollows and layered relief, potentially using an available box or mould/model.
- **Jewellery:** translate a discarded, body-shaped treatment object into a personal keepsake or wearable form, beginning with a non-worn replica study.

The note connects economic, personal/bodily and social costs to [body as project](/CostOfSmile/research/body-as-project-working-framework/). It includes verified museum/artist-interview references for Ana Mendieta, Shaun Leane for Alexander McQueen and Marc Quinn, clearly distinguished from direct precedents for aligner reuse.

**Status: Proposed / Planned. Idea only; not yet fabricated.** No prototype, cutting trial, wearable test or new material photograph has been completed for this entry.

Material composition remains unconfirmed: PETG/TPU or another polymer cannot be assumed from appearance or brand generalisations. The experiment records sharp-edge, fracture, hygiene, display and wearing risks. Initial studies should use cleaned retired aligners for non-worn display, suitable alternative transparent materials or replicas; untreated mouth-contact objects should not be worn directly, and cleaning alone does not establish wearable safety.

The next step is an inventory and real material photographs, followed by separate landscape and jewellery maquettes after material checks. Photos can be added later through Pages CMS. The [existing material archive](/CostOfSmile/materials/used-aligners-and-packaging/) remains the starting point.
