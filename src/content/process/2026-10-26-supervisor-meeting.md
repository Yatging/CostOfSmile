## What was confirmed

The registration system content was appropriate and has been submitted. The supervisor considered the central idea strong and encouraged the project to continue.

## What needs to change

The existing proposal and display tests focus too heavily on **economic cost**. Money remains one layer of the project, but it cannot carry the whole meaning of “cost.” The work now needs to develop two further layers:

1. **Personal and bodily cost** — pain, time, repeated inconvenience, appointments, eating and cleaning routines, and the demand to manage the body every day.
2. **Social cost** — peer pressure, aesthetic norms and the expectation that a smile should be corrected or improved.

The theory research also needs to widen. It should not remain limited to theoretical texts; comparable artworks and artistic methods must become part of the research archive.

## Translating the existing archive

Sticky notes are not yet an adequate form for the small inconveniences of treatment. They name experiences but do not transform them into an artistic language. Directly displaying clinical photographs would also be too literal.

The next step is to review the materials already available and test how their structures—time, repetition, reminders, waiting, stages and self-discipline—can become visual, spatial, digital or time-based work. The outcome does not need to remain a purely physical installation.

## Materials currently available

- A digital calendar recording each aligner change.
- WeChat appointment conversations with the dentist.
- Used aligners and related packaging.
- Possible access, through the clinic, to dental models, 3D scans or ClinCheck materials.

## Immediate action

Build a process website now, both to show sustained effort at final assessment and to let the supervisor follow the project as it develops. This record marks the beginning of that public process archive.
